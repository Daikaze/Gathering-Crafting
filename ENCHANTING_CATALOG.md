# Enchanting Reference — Gathering & Crafting v1.3.5

All 107 Enchantments are available with the required physical tools, a suitable Item, and sufficient Item enchantment slots. **Gold is an estimated market price / service value**, not a crafting material. Using the Enchanting tab never deducts coins from the maker, charges training tuition, or automatically transfers GP to a merchant. The game master may roleplay customer payments separately.

## Tool-only crafting (v1.3.5)

The only skill/tool prerequisite is a **physical Jeweler's Tools or Enchanter's Tools Item** in the character inventory. The tool may be identified by its name, baseItem/identifier, or a Gathering & Crafting toolKey flag.

- Physical tool present, without proficiency: normal Arcana check.
- Physical tool present, with matching tool proficiency: **Advantage** (roll 2d20, keep highest).
- No physical tool: Enchanting cannot start.
- No Enchanting rank training or separately learned formula is required. The Novice–Master tiers in this list are **item difficulty tiers**, used with rarity/slot limits, not mandatory character skill ranks.
- For new jobs, completion requires Arcana against a GM-only DC of 10 + 2 × formula tier. Failed check: another world day of work before retry. Jobs begun on older versions retain old completion rules.
- Market value is the price of the enchantment for customers; no gold is automatically deducted.

## Item limits

- Common: 1 enchantment slot, maximum formula Rank 2.
- Uncommon: 2 slots, maximum Rank 3.
- Rare: 3 slots, maximum Rank 4.
- Very Rare: 4 slots, maximum Rank 5.
- Legendary / Artifact: 5 slots, maximum Rank 5.
- GM may override item capacity to 0–6 slots from the Enchanting tab.
- Selected elite formulas require 2 slots. Duplicates, mutual exclusions, and another pending job on the same item remain blocked.
- Weapons: melee, ranged, blade, bow, crossbow, staff, polearm, bludgeoning and piercing tags. Armor: light, medium, heavy and shields.
- A pending enchantment created before v1.3.5 retains its original completion date and rules. Earlier spending is not retroactively refunded.

## Item type restrictions (v1.3.3)

**Every Enchantment has an explicit "Compatible with" list.** The workshop detects the actual dnd5e Item type and checks the same whitelist when beginning an enchantment. Incompatible choices are shown but locked; there is no fallback that allows any enchantment on any equipment.

- **Weapons:** melee or ranged weapons, swords, daggers, axes, hammers, maces, spears/polearms, bows, crossbows, staves, piercing, bludgeoning or heavy weapons, as appropriate to each recipe.
- **Armor:** Light, Medium and Heavy Armor are distinct equipment types. Shield enchantments require an actual shield. Robes are separate from armor and never silently count as Heavy Armor.
- **Wearables:** Robes, Boots, Cloaks, Gloves, Helmets, Rings, Amulets and Belts can have specialized formulas. A robe or accessory must be an actual dnd5e \`equipment\` Item: a \`loot\` item with "robe" in its name is not eligible. Recognized equipment subtypes and item names provide the classification.
- Existing enchantment flags and pending jobs remain unchanged, and the original elemental types remain compatible with previously learned recipes.

## DC visibility and automated behavior

Target DCs are displayed only to GMs in the Gathering, Alchemy, Blacksmithing and Enchanting interfaces, and detailed check messages are sent as GM whispers. Players see outcomes and their check totals without the target DC. Item names, market values, durations and compatibility remain visible to players.

Enchanting uses Foundry **world time** (one day = 86,400 world-time seconds), but never advances it automatically. Native +1/+2 item bonus, selected elemental damage and supported Active Effects can be written on the Item. Enchantment power Use buttons track once-per-Long-Rest uses. Advanced spells, reactions and conditional effects still require GM adjudication.
